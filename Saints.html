SAINTS

Sixteen witnesses from the time of Jesus

This collection introduces sixteen saints and holy people whose lives are connected with the time of Jesus. It includes Mary and Joseph, the family and friends of Jesus, people who encountered Him, and faithful witnesses named in the Gospels. The ten additional people after the first six are not Apostles.

Dates from the first century are often uncertain. Where Scripture and reliable historical sources do not give a birth or death year, this page says unknown rather than presenting a guess as fact.

M
01 · First

St. Mary — The Blessed Virgin

Mary was the mother of Jesus. The Gospels remember her faith, humility, courage and presence at key moments in Christ’s life, including the Crucifixion.

Feast: 1 January (Mary, Mother of God); 15 August (Assumption)

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Not historically established. Catholic teaching celebrates her Assumption; traditions differ on the details of her earthly end.

Why remembered: Her “yes” to God and her unique role as the mother of Jesus (Luke 1:26–38).

J
02 · Second

St. Joseph

Joseph was the husband of Mary and the earthly guardian of Jesus. A carpenter or craftsman by tradition, he protected and cared for the Holy Family.

Feast: 19 March; St. Joseph the Worker — 1 May

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; Christian tradition generally holds that he died before Jesus began His public ministry.

Why remembered: Faithfulness, humility, obedience and care for the Holy Family (Matthew 1–2).

▶ Play profile
J
03

St. John the Baptist

A prophet and preacher who called people to repentance and baptised Jesus in the Jordan River. He prepared the way for Christ.

Feast: 24 June (Nativity); 29 August (Passion)

Life: Late first century BC–about AD 28–30 (approximate).

Death: About AD 28–30; executed under Herod Antipas.

Why remembered: He recognised Jesus as the one who was to come and courageously spoke the truth (Mark 1:1–11; Matthew 14:1–12).

▶ Play profile
M
04

St. Martha of Bethany

Martha was the sister of Mary and Lazarus. She welcomed Jesus into her home and made a profound confession of faith before Lazarus was raised.

Feast: 29 July (with Mary and Lazarus)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Hospitality, practical service and her confession that Jesus is the Messiah (Luke 10:38–42; John 11:1–27).

▶ Play profile
M
05

St. Mary of Bethany

Martha’s sister and Lazarus’s sister. Mary sat at Jesus’ feet to listen to His teaching and anointed Him before His Passion in the Gospel account.

Feast: 29 July (with Martha and Lazarus)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Devotion to Christ, attentive discipleship and an act of love before His death (Luke 10:38–42; John 12:1–8).

▶ Play profile
L
06

St. Lazarus of Bethany

Lazarus was the brother of Martha and Mary. Jesus raised him from the dead, a sign that points to Christ as “the resurrection and the life”.

Feast: 29 July (with Martha and Mary)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: The Gospels do not record the year or circumstances of his later death.

Why remembered: His raising from the dead and the faith expressed by his sisters (John 11:1–44).

▶ Play profile
M
07

St. Mary Magdalene

A devoted follower of Jesus who witnessed the Crucifixion and was the first witness of the risen Christ in John’s Gospel. Jesus sent her to tell the disciples.

Feast: 22 July

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; later accounts are traditions, not securely established history.

Why remembered: Faithful discipleship and proclaiming the Resurrection (John 20:1–18).

▶ Play profile
E
08

St. Elizabeth

Elizabeth was the wife of Zechariah and the mother of John the Baptist. She welcomed Mary and recognised the blessing of the child Mary carried.

Feast: 5 November in the Roman Martyrology

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Her faith, gratitude and greeting to Mary (Luke 1:5–25, 39–45).

▶ Play profile
Z
09

St. Zechariah

Zechariah was a priest and the father of John the Baptist. The angel Gabriel announced that Elizabeth would bear a son; Zechariah later praised God.

Feast: 5 November in the Roman Martyrology

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; later stories about his death are not established by the Gospels.

Why remembered: His priestly service and the prophetic Benedictus (Luke 1:5–25, 57–79).

▶ Play profile
S
10

St. Simeon

A righteous and devout man in Jerusalem who, guided by the Holy Spirit, recognised the infant Jesus in the Temple and praised God.

Feast: 3 February in some Western calendars; Simeon the God-receiver is commemorated on 3 February in the Byzantine tradition.

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: His recognition of Jesus as God’s salvation (Luke 2:25–35).

▶ Play profile
A
11

St. Anna the Prophetess

An elderly widow and prophetess who worshipped in the Temple. She gave thanks to God and spoke about the child Jesus to those awaiting redemption.

Feast: 3 February in some Eastern Christian calendars

Life: First century BC–first century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Prayer, perseverance and recognising the Messiah (Luke 2:36–38).

▶ Play profile
N
12

St. Nicodemus

A Pharisee and member of the Jewish council who came to Jesus by night, later defended fair treatment for Him, and helped prepare His body for burial.

Feast: 31 August (Roman Martyrology, with Joseph of Arimathea)

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: His search for truth and his eventual public service at Jesus’ burial (John 3:1–21; 7:50–52; 19:39–42).

▶ Play profile
J
13

St. Joseph of Arimathea

A respected council member and secret disciple of Jesus who courageously asked Pilate for Jesus’ body and provided a tomb for His burial.

Feast: 31 August in the current Roman Martyrology

Life: First century AD; from Arimathea; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Courage and reverence in caring for Jesus after the Crucifixion (Matthew 27:57–60; John 19:38–42).

▶ Play profile
J
14

St. Joanna

Joanna was the wife of Chuza, an official in Herod’s household. She supported Jesus’ ministry and was among the women who went to the tomb.

Feast: 24 May in the Roman Martyrology; 27 June in some Eastern calendars

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Generosity, faithful service and witness to the Resurrection (Luke 8:1–3; 24:1–10).

▶ Play profile
S
15

St. Susanna

Susanna is named among the women who travelled with Jesus and the Twelve, helping to support them from their resources.

Feast: No universally observed individual feast day is established in the Roman calendar.

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown.

Why remembered: Quiet generosity and support for Jesus’ ministry (Luke 8:1–3).

▶ Play profile
C
16

St. Cleopas

Cleopas was one of the two disciples who met the risen Jesus on the road to Emmaus. They recognised Him when He broke the bread.

Feast: 25 September

Life: First century AD; exact years unknown.

Death: Unknown; no reliable historical year is recorded.

Why remembered: His encounter with the risen Christ and recognition of Him in the breaking of bread (Luke 24:13–35).

Historical note: These are traditional devotional depictions, not photographs of the people themselves.
