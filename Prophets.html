PROPHETS

Voices of faith, covenant and God's message

Meet three foundational figures in the biblical story: Abraham, Moses and Isaiah. Their lives and messages shaped the faith of Israel and remain important to Jewish and Christian traditions.

Each profile includes a curated gallery of traditional artistic depictions, a short biography, key themes and passages for further reading. The artwork is devotional or historical interpretation—not a verified portrait of the person. Open each artwork source to review its creator and reuse information.

01 · Patriarch

Abraham

Abraham is remembered as the patriarch of faith. In Genesis, God calls him to leave his homeland and promises to make his descendants a great nation through whom all families of the earth will be blessed.

Known for: Faith, covenant, obedience and hospitality.

Life: Placed in the ancestral period of Genesis; an exact historical date is not established.

Key moment: God's covenant with Abraham and the promise of descendants (Genesis 12:1–3; 15; 17).

Why remembered: He trusted God's promise and became a central model of faith (Genesis 15:6; Romans 4:1–22; Hebrews 11:8–12).

Scripture: Genesis 12–25; Matthew 1:1–2; Romans 4.

02 · Prophet and leader

Moses

Moses led the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt. According to the biblical account, God called him at the burning bush, worked through him in the Exodus, and gave Israel the covenant and the Law at Mount Sinai.

Known for: The Exodus, the covenant, the Ten Commandments and leadership.

Life: Traditionally placed in the second millennium BC; the precise date of the Exodus remains debated.

Key moment: Receiving the covenant at Mount Sinai (Exodus 19–20; 24).

Why remembered: Scripture describes Moses as a uniquely faithful servant of God and a prophet (Deuteronomy 34:10; Numbers 12:6–8).

Scripture: Exodus 2–20; Deuteronomy 5–34; Hebrews 11:23–29.

03 · Major prophet

Isaiah

Isaiah was a prophet in Judah who spoke during the reigns of several kings. His message called the people back to justice and faithfulness, warned against empty worship, and offered hope of restoration and peace.

Known for: Prophetic calls to justice, holiness, repentance and hope.

Ministry: Traditionally dated to the eighth century BC, during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah (Isaiah 1:1).

Key vision: Isaiah's vision of God's holiness and his call to serve (Isaiah 6:1–8).

Why remembered: His writings contain enduring promises of comfort, peace and the coming servant of the Lord (Isaiah 9:1–7; 11:1–9; 40:1–11; 52:13–53:12).

Scripture: The Book of Isaiah, especially chapters 1, 6, 9, 11, 40 and 52–53.

Historical and biblical note: Abraham is primarily described as a patriarch, though Genesis 20:7 calls him a prophet. Moses is explicitly identified as a prophet in Deuteronomy 34:10. Isaiah is one of the major writing prophets. Dates for Abraham and Moses are debated; the eighth-century BC setting for Isaiah's ministry is based on Isaiah 1:1.
